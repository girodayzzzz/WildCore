Outdoor ethics

Leave no trace.

The best outdoor experiences leave memories, not damage. A few thoughtful habits help protect the places we love.

Every landscape has its own pressures, rules and needs. Responsible outdoor recreation starts before arrival: learn about the area, choose suitable access points and understand what activities are allowed.

Plan ahead and prepare

Check local guidance, weather, seasonal restrictions and access conditions. Bring the right equipment so you do not need to improvise in ways that could damage the environment.

Stay on suitable routes

Use marked paths and designated areas where required. Avoid trampling sensitive vegetation, disturbing nesting areas or creating new shortcuts.

Take everything back

Pack out all rubbish, including food scraps and small items. Do not leave packaging, fishing line, cigarette ends or biodegradable waste behind; decomposition can take much longer than expected and wildlife may be harmed.

Respect wildlife and other people

Observe animals from a distance, never feed them and keep dogs under control where permitted. Keep noise low and give other visitors room to enjoy the outdoors.

Be careful with fire and water

Follow current fire restrictions and use only permitted facilities. Never leave a fire unattended. Keep soaps, fuels and other pollutants away from waterways and dispose of waste water according to local rules.

A simple rule

If you are unsure whether an action is allowed or safe, pause and check the official local guidance first.
