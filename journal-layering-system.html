The Outdoor Layering System Explained

Understand base, insulating and outer layers, and learn when to add or remove clothing on the trail.

The layering system is a flexible way to manage heat, sweat, wind and rain. Instead of relying on one very warm jacket, combine a few layers that each have a clear job. Adjust them as your pace, exposure and weather change.

1. Base layer: manage moisture

The layer next to your skin helps move sweat away so it can evaporate. Synthetic fabrics and merino wool are common choices. Cotton can hold moisture and become uncomfortable or cold when wet, especially in cool conditions. Choose a fit that is comfortable and allows movement.

2. Mid layer: retain warmth

Fleece, wool and insulated jackets trap warm air and help retain body heat. The right amount depends on temperature, wind, activity and how quickly you run warm or cold. A lighter layer that can be added or removed is often more versatile than one bulky garment.

3. Outer layer: block weather

A shell helps protect against wind and rain. Some shells are waterproof and breathable; others prioritise wind resistance or packability. Match the garment to expected conditions and remember that no fabric replaces good judgement in severe weather.

4. Adjust before you overheat

On an uphill climb, open zips or remove a layer before you sweat heavily. Add insulation when you stop, because your body produces less heat while resting. Keep a dry layer protected in your pack and pay attention to hands, head and feet.

A simple example

  • Mild, active hike: lightweight base layer and a shell in the pack.
  • Cool, breezy day: base layer, fleece and windproof shell.
  • Cold or changeable conditions: add suitable insulation, spare dry clothing, hat and gloves.
