SURVIVAL & BUSHCRAFT

How to Build a Basic Survival Kit

A useful emergency kit is compact, personal and familiar. Build it around realistic risks and the skills you have practised.

A survival kit is a backup for unexpected situations, not a substitute for planning, training or sound decisions. The contents should reflect the environment, season, group size and distance from help. Start simple and learn each item before relying on it.

1. Start with the priorities

Think about protection from exposure, access to safe water, basic first aid, communication and the ability to signal for help. The exact balance changes with the trip. A short local walk and a remote multi-day route do not need identical kits.

2. Shelter and warmth

Consider an emergency bivvy or suitable emergency shelter, plus insulation appropriate to the season. Keep it packed so it can be reached quickly. Learn how to use it before heading out, including how to protect yourself from wind and ground moisture.

3. Water and treatment

Carry drinking water and, where appropriate, a treatment method suited to likely sources. Filters, boiling and chemical treatments have different limits. Some do not remove chemical contaminants or every pathogen. Follow product instructions and use a known safe source whenever possible.

4. First aid and personal medication

Pack basic dressings, blister care, gloves and other supplies relevant to your activity. Add personal medication and check expiry dates. Do not include items you cannot safely use; consider recognised first-aid training.

5. Light, signalling and communication

A headlamp with spare power, a whistle and a charged phone are useful starting points. Keep emergency contacts and route details accessible. For remote trips, research appropriate satellite or emergency communication options and understand their coverage.

6. Small repair and utility items

A little repair tape, cord, a compact multitool where lawful and appropriate, and a fire-starting method only where legal and safe may help. Store these items securely and practise with them in controlled conditions. Never start a fire where restrictions or conditions make it unsafe.

7. Pack, test and review

Use a water-resistant pouch and organise items by purpose. Check the kit before each trip, replace used or expired supplies and adjust it when the season or activity changes. Practise simple scenarios with a responsible instructor or outdoor group.

A sensible rule

Do not let a kit create false confidence. Check the route and forecast, leave a trip plan with someone, know when to turn back and contact emergency services when the situation requires it.

WILDCORE field note

Conditions, access rules and local regulations can change. Check current official information, adapt this guidance to your environment and choose the safer option whenever in doubt.
