FREE STARTER PATH

Outdoor fundamentals

A first learning path covering preparation, navigation, shelter, water and responsible decisions.

MODULE 01

Plan before you go

Route research, weather, communication, timing and turnaround decisions.

Open lesson 1: Build a trip plan

Define the route, distance, elevation, expected duration and escape options. Check official access information and current forecasts. Share your route, group details and return time with a trusted person. Set a turnaround time before departure and revise the plan if conditions change.

Open lesson 2: Before departure

Check access, daylight, group capability and communication coverage. Carry a backup plan and agree on what conditions will make you cancel or turn back.

MODULE 02

Navigation essentials

Map symbols, contour lines, compass bearings and position checks.

Open lesson 2: Map and compass

Learn the map legend, scale, grid and contour interval. Match landforms to the terrain around you. Keep the compass level and away from metal or electronics. Navigate in short legs between recognisable features, and stop if the landscape no longer matches the map.

Full map & compass guide →
MODULE 03

Shelter and campsite

Choose safer ground, understand exposure and protect your sleep system.

Open lesson 3: Choose a site

Avoid dry streambeds, flood-prone hollows, unstable slopes and dead branches overhead. Check whether overnight stays and fires are permitted. Use durable surfaces and keep waste and washing away from water sources.

Full campsite guide →
MODULE 04

Water and fire awareness

Understand contamination risks, treatment choices and fire restrictions.

Open lesson 4: Water is not automatically safe

Clear water may contain pathogens. Use a treatment method appropriate to the likely hazards and follow the manufacturer's instructions. Boiling, filtration and disinfection have different strengths and limitations. Check local fire bans and never leave a permitted fire unattended.

Water treatment guide →
MODULE 05

Weather and first-aid readiness

Read changing conditions, carry a suitable first-aid kit and know when to seek help.

Open lesson 5: Prepare for changing conditions

Check more than one reliable forecast and understand that conditions can change quickly with terrain and altitude. Pack clothing for the expected conditions plus a dry insulating layer. Carry a trip-appropriate first-aid kit, know how to use its contents and make sure someone knows your route and expected return. In an emergency, contact local emergency services and follow their instructions.

First-aid kit guide →
MODULE 06

Shelter and exposure management

Choose a suitable site, reduce exposure and understand the limits of lightweight emergency shelter.

Open lesson 6: Choose a safer shelter site

Avoid dry stream beds, flood channels, unstable slopes, falling branches and exposed ridgelines in storms. Consider wind direction, drainage, ground conditions and local rules. An emergency shelter is not a substitute for a suitable sleep system or a safe decision to turn back.

Campsite selection guide →
MODULE 07

Leave no trace and wildlife awareness

Reduce impact, protect habitats and keep wildlife encounters safe and respectful.

Open lesson 7: Minimise your impact

Stay on permitted paths, pack out all waste, keep noise low and never feed wildlife. Give animals room to move away. Respect seasonal closures, private property and protected-area rules. Avoid sharing sensitive locations of nesting or rare species.

Leave no trace guide →
MODULE 08

Communication and emergency decisions

Share a realistic itinerary, plan check-in times and prepare for loss of signal.

Open lesson 8: Make your plan findable

Tell a trusted person your route, group size, vehicle location and expected return time. Agree what they should do if you miss the check-in. Keep emergency contacts accessible offline, conserve battery and do not rely on mobile coverage. If conditions deteriorate, turn back early rather than waiting for a crisis.

Preparedness guide →
MODULE 09

Food, hydration and field hygiene

Plan familiar food, carry enough water for the conditions and keep eating and hygiene routines practical.

Open lesson 9: Keep essentials manageable

Choose food you already know how to prepare and that suits the trip length, temperature and group. Carry water in clean, suitable containers and plan reliable refill points. Natural water is not automatically safe because it looks clear; use a treatment method appropriate to the suspected hazard and follow its instructions. Keep hands, utensils and food protected from contamination, pack out waste and account for allergies, medication, children and individual needs.

Review water safety →
MODULE 10

Equipment care and trip review

Check critical equipment before departure, maintain it after use and turn lessons from each trip into better preparation.

Open lesson 10: Close the loop

Before leaving, inspect footwear, shelter, closures, batteries, lights, first-aid supplies and navigation backups. Do not take damaged or unfamiliar equipment into demanding conditions without checking it first. After the trip, dry and clean gear according to the manufacturer's instructions, replace used supplies and record what worked, what was missing and what should change next time. Store equipment so it is ready for inspection, not merely out of sight.
