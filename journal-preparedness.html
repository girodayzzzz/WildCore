Outdoor Preparedness: The Essentials

Build confidence with a realistic plan, practical navigation skills and clear decisions about when to turn back.

Preparedness is not about carrying the most equipment. It is about understanding the trip, recognising changing conditions and having a few reliable ways to make good decisions.

Start with a realistic plan

Choose an objective that matches your experience, fitness and available time. Check the forecast, daylight hours, access restrictions and any local warnings. Share your route and expected return time with someone you trust.

Navigation is a skill

Learn to read the map for the area and understand the route before you leave. Carry a charged phone with offline maps, but keep a suitable backup and know how to use it. Batteries fail, devices get wet and signal can disappear.

Prepare for the conditions

Pack water, food, suitable layers, lighting and basic first aid according to the route and duration. Protect essential items from rain and keep a reserve for delays. Do not assume that a short trip cannot become a long one.

Know when to turn back

Set simple decision points before the trip: a latest turnaround time, weather limits and conditions that mean stopping or changing the route. Turning back is a sound outdoor decision, not a failure.

Build skills gradually

Practise navigation, basic first aid and equipment use in low-risk settings before relying on them in remote places. Take a recognised course where appropriate and follow local guidance.

WILDCORE field note

Conditions and local rules can change. Check current official information, adapt this guidance to your environment and choose the safer option whenever in doubt.
