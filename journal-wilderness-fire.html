How to Build a Campfire Safely

A practical guide to choosing a permitted fire site, keeping flames controlled and extinguishing every ember.

A campfire can be memorable, but it is never essential. Before collecting wood or striking a match, check whether fires are permitted at the exact site and on that date. Seasonal bans, drought, wind and local park rules take priority over any general guide.

1. Choose a safe, permitted place

Use an established fire ring where provided. Keep the fire well away from tents, dry grass, roots, low branches and other combustible material. Avoid exposed, windy places and never light a fire during a ban or when conditions are dry or gusty. Do not create a new fire pit where digging or ground disturbance is prohibited.

2. Prepare before lighting

Keep water and a suitable way to extinguish the fire within immediate reach. Use only permitted, locally sourced or supplied firewood; moving wood between areas can spread pests and disease. Gather only where local rules allow it. Never use petrol, alcohol or other accelerants.

3. Keep it small and attended

Start with a modest amount of dry tinder and kindling, then add small pieces of fuel gradually. A small fire is easier to control and needs less wood. Keep children and pets at a safe distance. One responsible adult should watch the fire continuously; never leave it unattended, even briefly.

4. Extinguish until completely cold

Allow enough time to put the fire out before leaving or going to sleep. Douse the embers thoroughly with water, stir the ash and wood, then add more water. Repeat until there is no steam, hissing or heat and every piece is cold to the touch. Do not simply cover hot embers with soil; buried embers can continue smouldering.

When in doubt, do not light it. Use a permitted stove, warm clothing and a headlamp instead.

Further reading: National Park Service — Minimize Campfire Impacts.

A note on responsible outdoor practice

This guide is general information, not a substitute for training or local official advice. Conditions and regulations vary. Check current rules for your exact destination.
